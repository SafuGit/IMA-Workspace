# Eval Cases — video-personalization

Qualitative checklist plus test prompts. This is a judgment-heavy skill (what counts as "a detail a person would know" is fuzzy by nature), so grade by eye rather than automated assertions.

## Checklist (grade each hook candidate against this)

- [ ] Could a normal viewer, watching once, plausibly notice this themselves? (the core filter)
- [ ] Does NOT require technical/domain expertise to appreciate
- [ ] Contains zero phrases from `references/ai-slop-blocklist.md`
- [ ] Is specific (names a moment, object, phrase, or opinion) rather than a vague compliment
- [ ] Reads in Safwan's voice (contractions, understated, not hyped — cross-check against `safwan-voice` skill)
- [ ] If a comment-backed hook exists, it's flagged as such, and the comment actually supports it (not a stretch)
- [ ] The recommended lead hook is the strongest, most specific one — not just the first one found

## Test prompts

1. Given a transcript + comments for a tech tutorial video, produce 2-3 hooks. Check none require the reader to know what the tool/library actually does.
2. Given a video with no available transcript (captions disabled), produce hooks from title + comments only, and confirm the output is flagged as lower-confidence.
3. Given a video where the top comments all react to a moment the transcript alone wouldn't flag as notable, confirm the skill catches it via the comment cross-check step.
4. Take a hook candidate deliberately written in AI-slop style ("this comprehensive video showcases a seamless workflow") and confirm the skill flags/rewrites it before it's used.
5. Given a video that's technically dense throughout (e.g. a deep systems-programming talk), confirm the skill still surfaces at least one accessible, non-technical hook rather than defaulting to a jargon-heavy one because "that's what the video is about."

## What "good" looks like

A brand or creator reading the resulting email should think "they actually watched this," not "they ran my video through a summarizer." If a hook could have been generated by skimming a transcript without watching a second of the video, it's not specific enough yet.
